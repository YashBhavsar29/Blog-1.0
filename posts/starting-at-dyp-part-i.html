Starting at DYP, Part 1: Before the Campus

Results, college lists, friends, forms, and a lot of CAP round drama.

This story goes back to 2025.

My diploma final exams were over, and the results were out. I’d scored 92.41%. It was a little lower than I had expected, but still good enough to land a decent college for my degree. 🎓

This is Part 1 of the story of how I started at DYP. It’s about everything before the campus: the lists, the forms, the friends and a lot of waiting. Part 2 will be about the first day and everything after.

GLOSSARYA quick glossary 📖

(for readers outside Maharashtra’s admission system)

  • CAP (Centralised Admission Process) The official process where students fill in a list of colleges and branches in order of preference. Seats are then allotted in rounds, based on marks, category and the seats available.
  • Cutoff The lowest marks at which a college gave a seat last year, for each branch and category.
  • DSY (Direct Second Year) After a three-year diploma, we can enter a degree directly in its second year.
  • Betterment Taking a seat in one round, but trying again in the next round for a better college.
  • Caste Validity A document needed for category-based seats.

THE RESULTSThe results

Until my results came, I hadn’t made a list of colleges. The same was true for my friends Mayur, Anand, Tushar, Shubham and the rest of the gang.

THE LISTSMaking the lists together 📊

We decided to sit together and build a college list for each person. But I’d done some research beforehand.

Using a few tools and Python scripts, I scraped all the college websites and analysed the previous year’s cutoff lists. From that I built an Excel sheet sorted by category, branch and college, with all the details a proper admission counsellor would give you. I sent it to our whole diploma classmates group.

data over guesswork.

Later, we used the same scripts and a few other tricks to make a separate options list for each person. We gathered at Mayur’s home a few times, and at Anand’s home a few times.

It was a chill period. We didn’t have much to do. We were relaxing, travelling and having fun. But underneath it all, there was a little anxiety. Would we end up in the same college or in different ones? Where would we go next? A lot of questions were running in our heads.

The same thing was going on in our other circle: Gautami, Pranjal and me. I filled the forms for both of them and helped them through the admission process, all the way to college selection.

MY LISTMy own CAP 1 list 📝

For CAP 1, my list had only 6 or 7 colleges: COEP, PICT, VIT, DYP Akurdi, DYP Pimpri and AISSMS. That was it. No other colleges, and only the Artificial Intelligence branches, no other departments.

only Pune. no hometown.

I’d already decided that this time I was going to step out of my comfort zone and leave my hometown to study, and specifically, go to Pune. I’d heard many times that Pune has far more opportunities than the other cities around us. Mumbai was also an option, but living in Mumbai isn’t easy, so I chose only Pune colleges. Not a single one from my hometown.

My relatives suggested I try government colleges. But I knew the reality: for a degree, only two or three government colleges are really good, the ones in Pune, Mumbai and Walchand, and those were far beyond my reach. So I’d already decided I wouldn’t settle for a government college in my hometown or nearby if I could get a better private college in Pune.

During that time, I watched many videos about college reviews and fees. That’s how I learned that VIT had slightly higher fees than the others, so I wasn’t sure whether to put it on the list at all. In the end, I did. Last year’s cutoff for that branch was already higher than my percentage, so I knew I probably wouldn’t get in. But I tried anyway, because I knew VIT’s student culture, activities and participation were great.

While filling the form, I was fairly sure that DYP Akurdi was the college I would get. I knew it in my heart, not as a feeling, but backed by data, because I’d studied the numbers of all the previous years.

THE FORMSThe form-filling guy 🧑‍💻

Because of all that data, and my helping nature, I became the person who filled everyone’s forms: Caste Validity forms, CAP forms and option forms. Tushar, Anand, Mayur, Shubham, Shivam, Shyam, Gautami and Pranjal. I did them all.

A few cases were harder than others.

Tushar and Shubham had lower marks, so a good college was never really on the table. In the first and second rounds, they didn’t get a college they were happy with either.

Gautami and Pranjal had a different problem: they wanted to go to the same college together if they were moving to another city. They were a little stuck to their comfort zone. I gave them my suggestions for CAP 1, but a friend of Gautami’s father, a teacher at a government college, filled both their forms on his own and didn’t listen to what they wanted. He filled in only government colleges across all cities. Since neither of them had marks good enough for a good government college, there was hardly any benefit in that.

CAP 1CAP 1 results 🎲

I got DYP Akurdi, exactly as I’d expected.

  • Mayur and Shivam got into the same college in Nashik.
  • Shubham and Tushar weren’t satisfied with what they got, so they went for betterment.
  • Anand got AISSMS and was happy with it.
  • Gautami and Pranjal got the same college together, but it was Government College Jalgaon, which I’d already warned them would happen. After asking a few seniors and teachers, they learned the college wasn’t good and was far out on the outskirts, so they were worried about what to do next.

They asked me to fill in their CAP 2 form. I filled it according to my research, but there was very little chance of them landing in the same college again. Hardly any seats were left in their category, since most get filled in round 1 itself.

Somehow, they both got colleges in Pune in the next round, but different ones. And since Pranjal’s Caste Validity still wasn’t printed, she had to go for round 3 without any real reason, and Gautami went along too.

Shivam was a little annoyed about going to Nashik. He’d wanted a college in Pune too. A few relatives and friends told him that the Robotics branch had a rising scope, so for round 2 he picked Robotics, and he landed in my college.

THE ONEThe one I didn’t want to see 😶

Then there was Diksha.

For personal reasons, and because of some incidents during diploma, I didn’t want to meet her, talk to her, or even see her. And she got into the same college as me.

I was very frustrated. Same college, same branch, maybe even the same class. How was I supposed to see, every single day, the face of a person I never wanted to see again?

After admissions, at least we ended up in different divisions. We didn’t talk after that. Sometimes we’d just pass each other like strangers.

THE SCATTERWhere everyone went 🗺️

By the end of the rounds, the gang had scattered:

  • Shubham got into a local college in our city.
  • Tushar didn’t get any change, and took admission in the same college he’d got.
  • Mayur and Anand took admission in their respective colleges.
  • Shyam just moved a wall apart, from our polytechnic campus to Deogiri College, right beside it.

PUNEMy admission day in Pune 🏫

I took admission in the first round.

It was my first visit to Pune in a long time. I’d been there before, but only for a family occasion, and I hadn’t gone anywhere except my relatives’ home. So it was almost a new city for me, with new people all around. For a kid who had always been shy and introverted, that was a big deal.

When I reached the campus, I was fascinated. I’d seen it in videos, but in reality it felt a little different.

That day, I met Vikas. (Yes, the same Vikas who later became Mahesh’s roommate, and our cameraman in the SIH story.) He was also taking admission and filling in the documents needed for loan applications, just like me. We had a short meeting, exchanged numbers, but didn’t really talk much.

Mahendra Kaka and Mama (my uncles) came with me for the admission process. While I was busy filling in my details on the college ERP portal, the hostel receptionist walked them through all the facilities of the college hostel, and they ended up paying a ₹10,000 advance booking fee.

When I came home, I told them many times: “Why did you book the hostel without asking me?” They didn’t listen.

THE MESSAGEThe unknown number 📱

I took admission in August and joined the DSY group. Curious about who else had taken admission, I compared the allotment list with the group members and checked their names on Truecaller to identify a few.

A few days later, a message suddenly appeared on my WhatsApp from an unknown number:

Hi, I am DSY, also from your college. — an unknown number

I assumed he’d got my number from the group. But I cross-checked, and his number wasn’t there, so it looked suspicious. I asked how he knew me. He said we’d met in a competition. I’d been in a lot of competitions here and there, so maybe. I asked where. He said Kolhapur.

That made me doubtful, because I’d never been to Kolhapur. I told him so.

He said he had a friend at DYP Akurdi who had taken admission and was pushing him to confirm his seat too, so he would confirm in the next round and come after that. I said nice, I’d be coming around then as well. He said maybe we could go together on our first day and sit together in class. I said “yeah,” a little doubtfully.

I shared the chat with Shivam and Tushar, about this unknown guy who says he knows me but I don’t know him. His name was Vinay. They said, “We’ll look at it afterwards. Just tell us if anything happens at college.”

THE STAYThe hostel trap and the PG hunt 🏠

Even though I took admission in round 1, I only started attending lectures after two or three weeks, because not many students were coming yet, and I also had to figure out where to live.

I was unhappy about the hostel. It had many facilities, but honestly I didn’t need them, and it felt like a trap: outside friends not allowed, no going out after 10 PM, and all food from the same mess every day, which is never a good thing. I needed a better alternative to make Kaka and Mama reconsider.

I asked my diploma seniors, Manaswi and Pranav Patil, both at the same college, if they knew any PGs or hostels nearby. They shared a few contacts. I called and enquired, but none seemed good or clean.

That doesn’t mean there weren’t any good PGs around. I just didn’t find one, because I had no friends other than those seniors to give me references. Later, after admission, I found many better alternatives. But by then, the time had gone.

THE PUZZLEThe Sanika and Monika puzzle 🧩

There was one more issue. Pranjal and Gautami had landed in different colleges in round 2.

I cross-checked the allotment list and found two girls, Sanika and Monika, who had taken the seats that kept my two friends apart:

  • Gautami could have joined Pranjal at AISSMS, but that seat was taken by Monika.
  • Pranjal could have joined Gautami, but that seat was taken by Sanika.

Both girls were unknown to us, and to each other.

So I had an idea: convince one of them to swap seats in the next round, so my friends could be together. The problem was how to reach them with just a name, and no phone number or contact details.

We searched on Google, used AI research, and a lot more. I found Sanika’s LinkedIn account, and Gautami found her on Instagram. Since Sanika also had MMCOE in hand, I asked Gautami to talk to her, with a warning: “Be polite, because if she doesn’t swap, you’ll be her classmate for the next three years.” First ask whether she was going to confirm admission, and if not, whether she’d consider AISSMS.

But Gautami didn’t feel comfortable talking to a stranger, so I tried too, through LinkedIn, trying to persuade her to consider AISSMS, since it was undeniably better than MMCOE. We only needed one of those two girls. But she didn’t listen. She was going for betterment, but only to PCCOE, because her sister studied there. We couldn’t change her mind.

Monika was the other option, but I couldn’t find her contact anywhere on the internet, except that she was from JSPM, because her name was mentioned in the college magazine. After many tries, we stopped searching.

But it stayed somewhere in the back of my head...

TAKEAWAYSWhat I took from this part 📌

  1. Do your homework early. The Excel sheet and the scripts didn’t just help me, they helped a whole friend group. Data won’t make the decision for you, but it removes the guessing.
  2. Know what you want before others decide for you. Gautami and Pranjal’s forms, and my hostel booking, were both decided by someone else, with good intentions. Speak up before the form is filled.
  3. Leaving your comfort zone starts long before the move. It started with one decision: only Pune.
  4. Not every plan works, and that’s okay. The swap didn’t happen, the PG hunt didn’t work. But both taught me something about asking, searching and letting go.
