Workless Days Are Reflection Days

No laptop, a long family function and a half-finished to-do list. Here’s what I did with the time.

There will be some days when you won’t be able to work. 💼👨‍💻

I know that sounds obvious. But if you’re someone who tracks everything, plans everything and wants to progress every single day, as I do, you know how heavy those days can feel.

THE TRIPThe trip to Nashik 🚂🧳

A year or two ago, I travelled to Nashik to visit my didi’s home. She’s my cousin: my father’s sister’s daughter. We went by train, and we stayed for 3 to 4 days.

The reason for the visit was a Vastu Shanti, a traditional ritual performed at a new home. It was a long family function, the kind with a lot of waiting, a lot of rituals and a lot of people talking. And honestly, I’ll admit it: this isn’t the kind of event I usually bother much about. 😅

I didn’t bring my laptop. So there was no project to open, no code to write, nothing to push to GitHub. I had only one thing with me: a paper journal.

THE GUILTDoing nothing, loudly 🤒

When you’re used to being busy, doing nothing is not quiet. It’s loud.

I sat there, free, and my brain started listing everything I could have been doing instead:

  • the projects I’d left half done,
  • the courses I’d started and stopped,
  • the things I’d told myself I would finish “soon.”

The more I thought about it, the more it felt like I was wasting time. The guilt arrived, the same guilt I get on any day I can’t progress.

doing nothing is loud.

We all carry a quiet rule in our heads: I should move forward every single day. But life doesn’t follow that rule. Sometimes it’s travel. Sometimes it’s sickness. Sometimes it’s a function, an event or a family occasion. 📆

THE PAUSEThen I opened the journal 📖

At some point, I took out the journal.

I didn’t plan a big session. I just started writing about what was going on in my life, and how I could make it better. A few minutes turned into a few hours, just me, a pen and the page.

And something shifted. I stopped seeing these days as lost days. I started seeing them as a pause: a chance to step back from day-to-day work, look at all the progress I’d already made, and ask where I was heading.

Around then, I remembered an Ali Abdaal video about prompts for reflecting on your life. So I sat down and wrote my way through them. ✍️

THE SHIFTWhat if workless days are reflection days? 🌠

That day gave me a new way to treat these days. Think of them as reflection days.

If you can’t move forward, you can still look around. Instead of using the day to feel bad, ask honest questions:

  • Were we doing our best?
  • Were we spending time wisely across every aspect of life, not only work?
  • Is there anything left behind?

These are the questions we never have time for when we’re busy. A workless day gives us the time. 😇

THE PROMPTSThe prompts from that video ✍️

These are the prompts I worked through. They come from Ali Abdaal’s video, and you can try them on any workless day:

  • 1. The Work Audit When it comes to work right now, what’s working and what’s not working?
  • 2. The Life Audit When it comes to my life right now, what’s working and what’s not working?
  • 3. The Truths Exercise What are 10 things that are true about you?
  • 4. Fulfillment Stories Think about a time in your life when you did something well and it brought you a sense of fulfillment.
  • 5. The Energy Audit Look back on your calendar or the activities you’ve done in the last two weeks and identify which ones gave you energy and which ones drained your energy.
  • 6. Truth Statements What are some truths that you’d like to be true about yourself 12 months from now?

You don’t have to answer them all. Pick one. Write for ten minutes. See what comes out.

My favourites are the two audits, because they ask a simple question: what’s working and what’s not? It’s amazing how rarely we stop to ask it. Most of the time we just keep doing what we’ve always done and hope it works.

The Energy Audit is a good one for people like me, who track everything. It uses something we already keep, our calendar, and turns it into a question: which of these things gave me energy, and which ones drained it?

THE HOW-TOHow to spend a workless day ❤️

You don’t have to turn it into a task. Some things that help:

  • Spend time with yourself. A walk, a window seat, a journal.
  • Spend time with family. Be where you are, with the people you came to see.
  • Let the day feel good. If it’s boring or guilty, you won’t reflect well.

And try to watch your life from a detached perspective, like someone looking at a story from the outside. How is it going? What’s working? What isn’t?

When you look at things from a distance, you come back to your work with a positive vibe and fresh energy. ⚡🙌 A rested mind often solves in ten minutes what a tired mind couldn’t solve in two hours.

THE TWISTThe unexpected part 🌱

I went to Nashik for a function I wasn’t excited about, with no laptop, no plan and no progress to show.

And yet it turned out to be time well used, because it was one of the few times I’d sat still long enough to listen to myself.

So if you have a day coming up when you can’t work, don’t dread it. 👇

BEFORE YOU GOSo make your workless days better 👍

  1. Let it go. A few missed days won’t break your progress.
  2. Pause. Don’t fill the gap with guilt.
  3. Reflect. Pick a prompt, grab a notebook, and write.
  4. Return fresh. Start again with a clear head.
Takeaway

Progress isn’t only the days we work. It’s also the days we step back, look at where we’re heading, and decide to keep going. 🌠

I hope it helps. 👍

— Yash
